# Provenance

This repository is the first public Git history for the standalone game and guided tutorial. The public package was prepared on 21 October 2026 from the authors' working copies.

The associated teaching deck, *Three heroes downhill: L-BFGS, ADMM and projected gradient, told without the math*, credits Gabriel Chua Yu Han and Mandar Chitre. The deck, its TeX source, and its research paper assets are not included here.

The deployed pages are reproducible from the files under `source/simulator/`:

- `source/simulator/page.html` and its JavaScript dependencies build `index.html`.
- `source/simulator/tutorial.html` and its JavaScript dependencies build `tutorial.html`.
- `source/simulator/advanced.html`, `keep.js`, `advanced_parts.js`, and `advanced_course.js` build `jade_keep_tutorial.html`.
- `source/examples/out/methods_numbers.tex` is retained only as a numerical test fixture.

The current 56-step teaching revision expands three mechanisms. The Cartographer's day-2 journal explanation has separate pages for the accepted step length, the stride, the two endpoint gradients, their change, and the rise-over-run quotient. The Twins sequence first separates the Wanderer's unconstrained minimization from the Warden's projection, then runs a rope-only counterfactual that settles with a fixed disagreement. It next introduces the scaled dual variable or ledger \(u\), derives the rope target or aim \(v=z-u\), and follows a complete ADMM update circuit. Finally, a nine-page self-checking light board replaces the proposed garden analogy: it grows a two-bit even-parity check into a 3 × 3 product code, separates received brightness from its channel LLR, introduces per-check replica vectors and per-edge scaled dual variables, and redraws the board as an LDPC-style graph. Its computed degree-three trace deliberately shows a zero syndrome before both ADMM residuals vanish. Running `python3 -B source/simulator/build_page.py` reproduces the pages whose current digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.

The initial publication passed all 41 numerical engine checks and all 21 checks of the lesson sequence. The current revision passes all 41 engine checks and all 59 lesson-sequence checks. Those checks include numerical contracts for the day-2 L-BFGS page, the stretched-map journal stride, the rope-only counterfactual, the first nonzero ADMM ledger entry, the next aim \(v=z-u\), every worked identity in the scaled form of ADMM, the light board's row and column syndromes, the unique evidence-selected codeword, the degree-three parity-polytope projection, the separation between syndrome success and residual convergence, and desktop and phone-stage drawing runs of all nine new pages.

The Jade Keep was added as a third, 28-step advanced tutorial from the same working source. Its current revision is an OFDM receiver lesson first and a parable second. It starts from the received OFDM frame, establishes the cyclic-prefix conditions for one-tap equalization, and separates residual-Doppler intercarrier interference from short-prefix intersymbol interference. Partial FFT is presented only as a supplied-deck front-end contrast, not as an input to the documented CAJODE engine. The tutorial then contrasts separate and joint receiver flows, shows the distinct roles of pilots and code constraints, introduces CAJODE's shared bit variables in the continuous relaxation and local ADMM copies, and follows the documented bit, channel, projection, scaled-dual, and candidate-test updates. The numerical engine is unchanged: the exact known-channel trace and the documented-equation joint toy still distinguish candidate acceptance, replica consensus, and complete-state settling. The current 86-check suite also verifies the teaching order, technical boundaries, accessibility hooks, and 140 sampled drawing states. The tutorial states that its demonstrated objective covers carrier coupling within the current OFDM symbol rather than a validated adjacent-symbol receiver, and that acceptance is not a global-optimality certificate. Its teaching content and numerical trace are unchanged in this release; only the shared responsive shell was corrected to stay inside the desktop viewport.

The teaching order was informed by the authors' working decks `joint_demodulation_decoding_merged.pdf`, `joint_demodulation_decoding_comic.pdf`, and `ici_and_isi_slides.pdf`. Those decks are not redistributed in this repository.

Two applied case studies were added on 22 October 2026 from the JadeJcade working copies. `localization_is_decoding.html` is built from `source/localization_decoding/`; its 24-step course separates physical admissibility, ADMM residual convergence, simulator-only accuracy, and the limits of the decoder analogy. Its 51 checks cover the synthetic sensors, projections, consensus residuals, stopping tests, teaching sequence, drawings, and standalone-page freshness. `finding_the_tear.html` is built from `source/torn_net/`; its 30-step course introduces image evidence and binary decisions before the position-copy consensus variables, and distinguishes targeted follow-up inspection from a CRC or convergence certificate. Its 41 checks cover the synthetic net, likelihood normalization, geometry projections, residual assembly, stopping claims, teaching sequence, drawings, and standalone-page freshness.

The localization and torn-net case studies reuse the byte-identical player in `source/simulator/board.js`. Their public build scripts point to that shared file rather than the private working-tree layout. The generated-page digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.

The training-plan case study was added on 23 October 2026 from the JadeJcade working copy. `the_coach_and_the_physio.html` is built from `source/training_plan/`; its 20-step, five-chapter course turns the day and load of an athlete's next hard session into an optimization problem. The Coach minimizes the predicted training cost, the Physio projects onto recovery, ramp, workload, taper, and nonnegative-load rules, and scaled ADMM coordinates their copies. Its 45 checks cover the readiness model, safe-set projection, ADMM identities and residual stopping, the multi-session outer loop, every drawing, teaching order, and standalone-page freshness. It reuses the byte-identical player in `source/simulator/board.js`, and its generated-page digest is recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.

The roads between the six pages were added on 23 October 2026. `source/simulator/realm.js` is inlined into every page by the four build scripts, and each template names its page with a `kings-land-page` meta tag. The shared player in `source/simulator/board.js` gained an interlude mode: a short list of steps played in place of the course, after which the course resumes at a given step; on the game page the interlude opens the board by itself and closes it at the end. The curated graph has 43 roads and 118 interlude steps: 25 contextually prominent roads, 10 folded comparisons, and 8 map-only links. Each starts only after its prerequisite concept and lands before the destination explanation it promises. A full interlude has the map of the King's land with the road walked, up to two scenes that set up the new place, and a key of what changes name. Cards state what is preserved and what changes, estimate the journey time, support direct opening, and offer full replay after an abbreviated repeat; end pages use ordered next-lesson choosers. Per-road and per-page-pair progress, prediction prompts, accessible status text, and nested return tickets preserve orientation without changing any page's teaching steps or numbers. `source/simulator/test_realm.js` checks this road behavior, and the six existing suites pass unchanged. The generated-page digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.
