# Provenance

This repository is the first public Git history for the standalone game and guided tutorial. The public package was prepared on 21 October 2026 from the authors' working copies.

The associated teaching deck, *Three heroes downhill: L-BFGS, ADMM and projected gradient, told without the math*, credits Gabriel Chua Yu Han and Mandar Chitre. The deck, its TeX source, and its research paper assets are not included here.

The deployed pages are reproducible from the files under `source/simulator/`:

- `source/simulator/page.html` and its JavaScript dependencies build `index.html`.
- `source/simulator/tutorial.html` and its JavaScript dependencies build `tutorial.html`.
- `source/simulator/advanced.html`, `keep.js`, `advanced_parts.js`, and `advanced_course.js` build `jade_keep_tutorial.html`.
- `source/examples/out/methods_numbers.tex` is retained only as a numerical test fixture.

The current 66-step teaching revision expands three mechanisms. The Cartographer's day-2 journal explanation has separate pages for the accepted step length, the stride, the two endpoint gradients, their change, and the rise-over-run quotient. The Twins sequence first separates the Wanderer's unconstrained minimization from the Warden's projection, then runs a rope-only counterfactual that settles with a fixed disagreement. It next introduces the scaled dual variable or ledger \(u\), derives the rope target or aim \(v=z-u\), and follows a complete ADMM update circuit. Finally, a ten-page self-checking light board replaces the proposed garden analogy: it grows a two-bit check that requires even parity into a 3 × 3 product code, separates received brightness from its channel LLR, introduces per-check replica vectors and per-edge scaled dual variables, draws one row's even-parity polytope inside its cube, and redraws the board as an LDPC-style graph. Its computed degree-three trace deliberately shows a zero syndrome before both ADMM residuals vanish. Running `python3 -B source/simulator/build_page.py` reproduces the pages whose current digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.

The initial publication passed all 41 numerical engine checks and all 21 checks of the lesson sequence. The current revision passes all 41 engine checks and all 79 lesson-sequence checks. Those checks include numerical contracts for the day-2 L-BFGS page, the stretched-map journal stride, the rope-only counterfactual, the first nonzero ADMM ledger entry, the next aim \(v=z-u\), every worked identity in the scaled form of ADMM, the light board's row and column syndromes, the unique evidence-selected codeword, the degree-three parity-polytope projection, the separation between syndrome success and residual convergence, and desktop and phone-stage drawing runs of all ten light-board pages.

The Jade Keep was added as a third, 28-step advanced tutorial from the same working source. Its current revision is an OFDM receiver lesson first and a parable second. It starts from the received OFDM frame, establishes the cyclic-prefix conditions for one-tap equalization, and separates residual-Doppler intercarrier interference from short-prefix intersymbol interference. Partial FFT is presented only as a supplied-deck front-end contrast, not as an input to the documented CAJODE engine. The tutorial then contrasts separate and joint receiver flows, shows the distinct roles of pilots and code constraints, introduces CAJODE's shared bit variables in the continuous relaxation and local ADMM copies, and follows the documented bit, channel, projection, scaled-dual, and candidate-test updates. The numerical engine is unchanged: the exact known-channel trace and the documented-equation joint toy still distinguish candidate acceptance, replica consensus, and complete-state settling. The current 86-check suite also verifies the teaching order, technical boundaries, accessibility hooks, and 140 sampled drawing states. The tutorial states that its demonstrated objective covers carrier coupling within the current OFDM symbol rather than a validated adjacent-symbol receiver, and that acceptance does not certify a global optimum. Its teaching content and numerical trace are unchanged in this release; only the shared responsive shell was corrected to stay inside the desktop viewport.

The teaching order was informed by the authors' working decks `joint_demodulation_decoding_merged.pdf`, `joint_demodulation_decoding_comic.pdf`, and `ici_and_isi_slides.pdf`. Those decks are not redistributed in this repository.

Two applied case studies were added on 22 October 2026 from the JadeJcade working copies. `localization_is_decoding.html` is built from `source/localization_decoding/`; its 24-step course separates physical admissibility, ADMM residual convergence, simulator-only accuracy, and the limits of the decoder analogy. The training-plan case study was added on 23 October 2026 from the JadeJcade working copy. `the_coach_and_the_physio.html` is built from `source/training_plan/`; its 20-step, five-chapter course turns the day and load of an athlete's next hard session into an optimization problem. The Coach minimizes the predicted training cost, the Physio projects onto recovery, ramp, workload, taper, and nonnegative-load rules, and scaled ADMM coordinates their copies. Its 45 checks cover the readiness model, safe-set projection, ADMM identities and residual stopping, the multi-session outer loop, every drawing, teaching order, and standalone-page freshness. The soldier tutorial was added on 23 October 2026. `bp_and_cajode.html` is built from `source/soldiers/` and draws on the authors' soldier slides (decks soldier_v2 and soldier_v3 of the CAJODE repository), which are not redistributed here. Its current 34 steps in eight chapters begin with the OFDM receiver question, teach messages that omit their recipient’s incoming value through a counting formation, and then use one connected, acyclic six-bit Tanner graph for both mechanisms. Sum-product BP carries a three-hop evidence wave across that tree before correcting its uniquely weakest channel decision. The known-channel CAJODE reduction uses the same channel LLR convention, shared relaxed bits, replica variables and one scaled dual variable per edge; its hard word passes after three circuits while the RMS consensus error remains nonzero. A related fixture closes the missing edge and uses different channel evidence to expose recirculation. An explicitly optional detour contrasts those retained constraint variables with projected gradient descent, box projection and backtracking on one penalty function, without treating update counts as a decoder ranking. The receiver chapter restores the channel. In the referenced JADE implementation, a BP decoder uses normalized min-sum at its check nodes to form a posteriori LLRs. The receiver adds α times the extrinsic information to the channel LLRs and maps the sum through tanh to initialize joint channel estimation and decoding on the next pass. CAJODE instead updates the channel inside each ADMM iteration. `source/soldiers/test_soldiers.js` has 59 checks covering the graph fixture, exact numerical traces, technical wording, prediction prompts, road-stable step IDs, desktop and phone drawings, and standalone-page freshness. The roads suite checks the surrounding interludes and links. The generated-page digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.

Later on 23 October 2026 two explanations in the introductory tutorial were revised. The Wanderer's step is now told as gradient descent on the land plus the rope, F(x) = f(x) + ρ/2·|x − v|². Before the walk, the page draws the land's pull −∇f(x) and the rope's pull ρ(v − x) at the start, lists both, and takes the first step along their sum. The walk then uses the stride 2/trace(A + ρI) and plots F after each step. A new page, ‘Where the walk stops: two pulls cancel’, shows the two pulls equal and opposite at the end of the walk. On the quadratic valley it turns that balance into the two linear equations that the Wanderer solves. A new light-board page, ‘Inside the cube: the even-parity polytope’, follows ‘One check keeps one replica’. It draws row R2's cube, its four even corners and the solid they span. It gives two reasons why the solid is convex. A point between two mixtures is again a mixture: a dot slides between two of them while its four weights stay at least 0 and add to 1. The solid is also the cube with each odd corner cut off by one flat cut. The page ends with R2's round-4 projection onto one face of the solid. The course then had 58 steps, and `source/simulator/test_learning_flow.js` had 66 checks. On a settled last circuit, the Wanderer's height plot no longer hangs the page. The shared player can keep a map label inside the map, and axis ticks now stop short of the axis names, so all seven pages were rebuilt.

On 23 October 2026 the Twins chapter gained six day-by-day pages, in the tutorial only. Each day the Wanderer faces a new land: the valley plus the rope's bowl around a spot made from the day before. With the rope alone that spot is yesterday's Warden, z; with the book it is the aim z − u. Each day page fades yesterday's land into today's and marks its lowest point. It then walks the Wanderer there and moves the Warden. With the book, it also writes the gap into the book. It ends on the spot that tomorrow's rope is tied to. Three pages show days 1 to 3 with the rope alone, before ‘The rope pulls, but does not remember’, which now continues from day 3 and states that the gap first opens on day 4. Three pages show days 6 to 8 with the book, after the detailed day-5 circuit; the fast page that follows replays days 4 to 12 and says so. ‘The Warden’s book: u’ now points to these pages. The game's lesson board is unchanged. The course has 64 steps, and `source/simulator/test_learning_flow.js` has 71 checks. They confirm that each day's Wanderer stops where A(x − c) + ρ(x − v) = 0 for that day's v, and that each day's v comes from the day before. The shared player now keeps the last four land grids, so a page can fade one land into another without recomputing either. Axis ticks also stay clear of a counter at the top-left of a map. All seven pages were rebuilt. `source/examples/twins_day_by_day.py` draws the same day-by-day cost functions with Matplotlib; its numbers match the engine's on every day of both runs.

Its 24 steps in five chapters define a recording–hydrophone case, a test condition, a receiver result and a pooled curve point before asking the reader to interpret the table. A mechanism map distinguishes the five complete receiver routes from BP as one component inside a route, and gives JADE, CORAL and CAJODE their implementation-specific roles. On 24 October 2026 the Warden's introduction in the Twins chapter grew from one page to three, in the tutorial only. Students could not follow the old page, which named c and Π without saying what they meant or how the Warden finds its answer. ‘Meet the Warden: z’ now defines the lawful set C, the shaded square, and says in words that Π_C(v) is the projection of v onto C, with Π the Greek capital letter pi. ‘The Warden’s problem, step by step’ hands the Warden c, the valley's floor, and solves the Warden's own minimization in two ways. A circle grows from c until it first reaches a lawful spot; then each coordinate is settled on its own number line. ‘Nearest is not lowest’ grows the land's elliptical rings from c until one touches the wall at the spot to dig, beside the Warden's circle. Its side plots show distance and height along the east wall, each with a different lowest point. The game's lesson board keeps the single page. Later pages now print Π_C with a subscript, as the new pages do. The course has 66 steps, and `source/simulator/test_learning_flow.js` has 79 checks. The new checks confirm that Π(c) is c clipped to the wall, with no nearer lawful spot on a fine grid. They also confirm that the land's ring through x* first touches the wall at x*, and that every quoted number is computed. The game page and the tutorial were rebuilt.
