# Provenance

This repository is the first public Git history for the standalone game and guided tutorial. The public package was prepared on 21 October 2026 from the authors' working copies.

The associated teaching deck, *Three heroes downhill: L-BFGS, ADMM and projected gradient, told without the math*, credits Gabriel Chua Yu Han and Mandar Chitre. The deck, its TeX source, and its research paper assets are not included here.

The deployed pages are reproducible from the files under `source/simulator/`:

- `source/simulator/page.html` and its JavaScript dependencies build `index.html`.
- `source/simulator/tutorial.html` and its JavaScript dependencies build `tutorial.html`.
- `source/simulator/advanced.html`, `keep.js`, `advanced_parts.js`, and `advanced_course.js` build `jade_keep_tutorial.html`.
- `source/examples/out/methods_numbers.tex` is retained only as a numerical test fixture.

The current 66-step teaching revision expands three mechanisms. The Cartographer's day-2 journal explanation has separate pages for the accepted step length, the stride, the two endpoint gradients, their change, and the rise-over-run quotient. The Twins sequence first separates the Wanderer's unconstrained minimization from the Warden's projection, then runs a rope-only counterfactual that settles with a fixed disagreement. It next introduces the scaled dual variable or ledger \(u\), derives the rope target or aim \(v=z-u\), and follows a complete ADMM update circuit. Finally, a ten-page self-checking light board replaces the proposed garden analogy: it grows a two-bit check that requires even parity into a 3 × 3 product code, separates received brightness from its channel LLR, introduces per-check replica vectors and per-edge scaled dual variables, draws one row's even-parity polytope inside its cube, and redraws the board as an LDPC-style graph. Its computed degree-three trace deliberately shows a zero syndrome before both ADMM residuals vanish. Running `python3 -B source/simulator/build_page.py` reproduces the pages whose current digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.

The initial publication passed all 41 numerical engine checks and all 21 checks of the lesson sequence. The current revision passes all 41 engine checks and all 86 lesson-sequence checks. Those checks include numerical contracts for the day-2 L-BFGS page, the stretched-map journal stride, the rope-only counterfactual, the first nonzero ADMM ledger entry, the next aim \(v=z-u\), every worked identity in the scaled form of ADMM, the light board's row and column syndromes, the unique evidence-selected codeword, the degree-three parity-polytope projection, the separation between syndrome success and residual convergence, and desktop and phone-stage drawing runs of all ten light-board pages.

The Jade Keep was added as a third, 28-step advanced tutorial from the same working source. Its current revision is an OFDM receiver lesson first and a parable second. It starts from the received OFDM frame, establishes the cyclic-prefix conditions for one-tap equalization, and separates residual-Doppler intercarrier interference from short-prefix intersymbol interference. Partial FFT is presented only as a supplied-deck front-end contrast, not as an input to the documented source engine. The tutorial then contrasts separate and joint receiver flows, shows the distinct roles of pilots and code constraints, introduces SAPPHIRE's shared bit variables in the continuous relaxation and local ADMM copies, and follows the documented bit, channel, projection, scaled-dual, and candidate-test updates. The numerical engine is unchanged: the exact known-channel trace and the documented-equation joint toy still distinguish candidate acceptance, replica consensus, and complete-state settling. The current test suite also verifies the teaching order, technical boundaries, accessibility hooks, and sampled drawing states. The tutorial states that its demonstrated objective covers carrier coupling within the current OFDM symbol rather than a validated adjacent-symbol receiver, and that acceptance does not certify a global optimum. Its teaching content and numerical trace are unchanged in this release; only the reader-facing receiver name and shared responsive shell changed.

The teaching order was informed by the authors' working decks `joint_demodulation_decoding_merged.pdf`, `joint_demodulation_decoding_comic.pdf`, and `ici_and_isi_slides.pdf`. Those decks are not redistributed in this repository.

The soldier tutorial was added on 23 October 2026. `bp_and_cajode.html` is built from `source/soldiers/` and draws on the authors' soldier slides (decks soldier_v2 and soldier_v3 of the legacy CAJODE source repository), which are not redistributed here. Its current 34 steps in eight chapters begin with the OFDM receiver question, teach messages that omit their recipient’s incoming value through a counting formation, and then use one connected, acyclic six-bit Tanner graph for both mechanisms. Sum-product BP carries a three-hop evidence wave across that tree before correcting its uniquely weakest channel decision. The known-channel SAPPHIRE reduction uses the same channel LLR convention, shared relaxed bits, replica variables and one scaled dual variable per edge; its hard word passes after three circuits while the RMS consensus error remains nonzero. A related fixture closes the missing edge and uses different channel evidence to expose recirculation. An explicitly optional detour contrasts those retained constraint variables with projected gradient descent, box projection and backtracking on one penalty function, without treating update counts as a decoder ranking. The receiver chapter restores the channel. In the referenced JADE implementation, a BP decoder uses normalized min-sum at its check nodes to form a posteriori LLRs. The receiver adds α times the extrinsic information to the channel LLRs and maps the sum through tanh to initialize joint channel estimation and decoding on the next pass. SAPPHIRE instead updates the channel inside each ADMM iteration. `source/soldiers/test_soldiers.js` has 59 checks covering the graph fixture, exact numerical traces, technical wording, prediction prompts, road-stable step IDs, desktop and phone drawings, and standalone-page freshness. The roads suite checks the surrounding interludes and links. The generated-page digests are recorded in `SHA256SUMS`.
